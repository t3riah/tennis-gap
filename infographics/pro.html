The 2 Primitives

Every tennis stat you have ever seen is built from two numbers. Start here, and the rest is arithmetic.

On serve

Service Points Won SPW

Share of points won on your own serve — first and second serves combined.

ATP AVG~64%
WTA AVG~56%
On return

Return Points Won RPW

Share of points won when the other player serves. The quieter half — and usually the separator.

ATP AVG~36%
WTA AVG~44%
SPW + RPW = Composite
Tour average is ~1.000 on both tours. A composite of 1.05 is a genuinely better-than-field player; 1.10+ is elite territory.

Why the Rule of 100 is limited

  • It averages to 100 by construction. One player's hold is the other player's failed break — so Hold % + Break % sums to ~100 across the field, just like the composite. “Over 100” means above average, nothing more absolute.
  • It's a games-level summary. Games are a non-linear squash of the points underneath — the score hides how the points were won.
  • It hides the mechanism. Two players can both hold 78% for completely different reasons — and break for different reasons next match.

The 3 Adjusters That Add Real Lift

The six numbers are the engine. These three decide whether the engine is reading the right player, in the right form, with the right confidence.

ADJUSTER / 01

Per-surface only — never overall

The six numbers on clay describe a different player than the same six on grass. Blending surfaces blends two players into a fiction. Always price the surface being played.

CLAYHARDGRASS— priced separately, always
ADJUSTER / 02

Opponent-adjusted, recency-weighted

Raw percentages against a weak draw lie. Correct for who the points were won against (surface Elo), then weight recent form so last month counts more than last year.

540-day

half-life on form — a result's weight halves every 540 days.

ADJUSTER / 03

Sample size + shrinkage

A number is only as good as the evidence under it. Thin data pulls hard toward the tour averages above; deep data is allowed to speak for itself. Every read carries its confidence with it.

05

Noise, Not Drivers

What TV graphics love and pre-match models mostly ignore. These describe style or outcomes — they don't drive wins, and break-point save % as a standalone stat mostly regresses back to the SPW/RPW that created the break point in the first place. Wind, a tired serve, lengthening rallies: noisy in-play modifiers, not pre-match drivers.

Why in-play “signals” mislead: 15 serves carry ~13 points of standard error — a two-game dip is usually just variance wearing a story.
